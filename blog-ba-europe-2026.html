Reflections from BA Europe 2026: The Future BA is More Human, Not Less

22.10.2026

There is something special about stepping away from the day-to-day pressures of work and spending a couple of days surrounded by people who genuinely care about Business Analysis. BA Europe 2026 did exactly that. It brought together Business Analysts, change leaders and curious newcomers to share ideas, challenge assumptions and remind us why we enjoy this profession.

Daniel Grist

Senior Consultant, CMC Partnership Consultancy

Beyond the agenda, what stayed with me most was the sense of community. The conversations between sessions, questions asked without fear of sounding inexperienced, and the shared recognition that we’re all still learning created an atmosphere that is difficult to replicate anywhere else.

Learning beyond the day job

One of the biggest benefits of attending a conference is perspective. In our normal roles, it is easy to become absorbed in our own organisations, projects and ways of working. At BA Europe, every coffee break became an opportunity to hear how colleagues in healthcare, finance, government, higher education and consultancy approach similar challenges.

The themes felt particularly timely. AI, data, product delivery and strategic influence featured heavily throughout the programme, reflecting how rapidly the BA role is evolving. Yet despite the focus on emerging technologies, a consistent message emerged.

The fundamentals still matter.

Understanding the problem. Listening carefully. Building trust. Challenging assumptions. Helping people make better decisions.

AI can help draft documentation, summarise information and accelerate parts of our work. What it cannot replace is judgement. It doesn’t understand organisational culture, stakeholder relationships or the nuances that often determine whether change succeeds or fails.

A community that gives back

For Young Business Analysts, the conference felt like an important checkpoint for the community. It was brilliant to meet apprentices, graduates, career changers and experienced professionals who wanted to support the next generation.

Those conversations reinforced why communities like YBA matter. They create opportunities for people to ask questions, share experiences and grow with support rather than in isolation.

A highlight was speaking with Hannah Burbridge, winner of the 2026 Business Analyst of the Year Award. Hannah’s achievement was undoubtedly deserved – her passion for the profession, her commitment to developing others and the genuine enthusiasm she brings to every conversation. Hannah has built and supported a national community of analysts, helping to raise the profile of Business Analysis while encouraging others to develop their careers. Speaking with her was a reminder that impact isn’t measured solely by the projects we deliver, but also by the people and communities we help grow.

Courage to contribute

Another recurring message throughout the conference was that you do not need to be an expert to contribute.

You need curiosity, lived experience and the courage to share what you have learned.

Many speakers openly discussed mistakes, lessons learned and challenges they had faced. These were often the most memorable sessions because they felt practical, authentic and relatable rather than polished theory.

That is an important lesson for young BAs. The question you ask in a workshop, the blog post you write, the mentoring conversation you have or the talk you eventually deliver could be exactly what somebody else needs to hear.

Growth often begins when we step slightly outside our comfort zone.

Celebrating the profession

The conference also gave us an opportunity to celebrate the people who continue to shape and strengthen our profession.

One particularly special moment was seeing Debra Paul receive the inaugural induction into the BA Hall of Fame. As a long-standing advocate for Business Analysis and one of Young Business Analysts’ proud supporters, the recognition felt both fitting and well deserved.

Many of us have learned from Debra’s writing, speaking and leadership over the years. Her contribution to the BA profession has influenced countless practitioners and helped elevate Business Analysis as a discipline.

Taking the energy forward

Leaving a conference is always bittersweet. The buzz fades, the inbox returns and reality quickly reasserts itself. But the real value lies in what we do next.

For me, the priorities are simple: revisit my notes, test a new idea in a real piece of work, reconnect with people I met and continue contributing to the YBA community.

BA Europe 2026 was a reminder that while our profession continues to evolve, its heart remains the same.

Curious people helping organisations and individuals move from what is, to what could be.
